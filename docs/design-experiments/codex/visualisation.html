Landing page

The product reads as warm and trustworthy, but the long scroll makes “what do I do first?” softer than it should be.

  • The tree mark, green accent, and centered hero already establish a recognizable care-first brand.
  • The ecosystem section is comprehensive, but action intent and proof are mixed into one long visual rhythm.
  • Buttons, cards, and headings rely on the same weight range, so hierarchy depends more on color than on structure.
Local capture Auth

Login page

The form itself is clean and focused, but the empty brand panel makes the desktop shell feel unfinished.

  • Phone-number-first entry suits a healthcare context and keeps the form compact.
  • The role context exists in copy, yet it is visually quieter than it should be for a dual-audience platform.
  • The left half holds atmosphere but very little meaning, so the page feels visually split rather than intentionally balanced.

Signup page

The role selector is immediately useful, but the emoji-driven role cards shift the tone away from the calmer product surfaces.

  • The first decision is visible early, which is good for a multi-tenant platform.
  • The role cards feel less precise than the rest of the brand and would benefit from more explicit hierarchy and explanation.
  • The structure is strong enough already that visual refinement alone could make this page feel much more intentional.

Patient Dashboard Deep Dive

The dashboard problem is hierarchy, not architecture.

The Angular structure is already workable: greeting, status, summary, services, records, care hero, and downstream actions. The deeper issue is that the top of the page does not make the care plan feel as important as the service catalog. That means the best redesign move is a visual one, not a functional rewrite.

What is already working
  • The dashboard feels approachable and light rather than intimidating.
  • The service taxonomy is clear, especially for first-time users.
  • The care hero copy is directionally right and aligns with the product promise of guided care.
Anti-pattern verdict

This does not feel like generic AI slop, but it is under-articulated. The page uses gentle surfaces and branded greens well, yet too many modules share the same softness, so the eye does not know what matters first.

Biggest friction points
  • The summary cards are too quiet, especially when values are zero, so the status layer feels disposable.
  • The service tiles are large and airy, which pushes the most helpful guided-care surface lower.
  • The records card and care hero do not visually belong to the same care journey even though they should.
  • The floating WhatsApp action and bottom nav pull some attention away from the main next-step story.
Priority visual moves while keeping the same structure
Compress the service field without removing anything.

Reduce tile height slightly, enlarge icon-title pairing, and let the care hero peek sooner into common laptop viewports.

Make the summary row read as status, not decoration.

Use denser numerals, clearer labels, and stronger category tinting so the patient immediately understands their current state.

Bridge records and care plan with one surface grammar.

Shared borders, tonal backgrounds, and consistent micro-headings can make those modules feel sequential instead of isolated.

Reserve the strongest contrast for guided action.

The CTA, care-plan signals, and section introductions should carry the boldest contrast, not every tile equally.
