# Scenario S3-O3-I2 — Our Level 3 (Fully Funded) / Investor Level 2 (Ideal)

## Scenario Summary

High-investment balanced path. The founders deploy capital at corporate intensity (~150-200% of ideal spend) through Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1, creating a polished product and operational foundation. An ideal-level investor arrives right after Pilot 1, providing properly sized capital to sustain and scale through Pilot 2 and Production Readiness. The result is a fast but not frantic execution — strong early foundation with adequate scaling capital.

- **Economic cost to Production:** $200,000-260,000 / INR 16,600,000-21,580,000
- **Target timeline to Production:** 16-22 months
- **Founder/company capital before investor:** $89,000-120,000 / INR 7,387,000-9,960,000
- **Investor capital modeled:** $111,000-140,000 / INR 9,213,000-11,620,000
- **Equity structure:** 60 / 20 / 20 (founder / founding team / investor pool)
- **Main risk to watch:** high founder capital exposure pre-investor ($89,000-120,000 in 3-5 months); if Pilot 1 fails to attract the investor, the founder absorbs a large sunk cost

## Recommendation

S3-O3-I2 is a **viable and potentially strong path**, though it carries higher founder capital risk than the primary recommendation (S3-O2-I2). It is suitable for founders with deep personal reserves who want to maximize the quality of their Pilot 1 demonstration.

**Strengths:**
1. The fully funded Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1 produce the strongest possible investor pitch — a polished product, live patients, real operations data
2. The ideal investor provides properly sized capital for Pilot 2 and Production Readiness
3. Timeline of 16-22 months is competitive, only slightly slower than S3-O3-I3
4. Risk level is Medium — manageable with proper execution and phase gates

**Weaknesses:**
1. Founder capital exposure is $89,000-120,000, roughly $36,000-55,000 more than S3-O2-I2
2. The extra founder investment does not proportionally reduce risk or improve timeline (diminishing returns from O2 to O3)
3. Late-stage cash gap requires either aggressive revenue or supplementary capital
4. If Pilot 1 fails to attract an investor, the founder has a large sunk cost

**When to pursue S3-O3-I2 over S3-O2-I2:**
- Founders have personal capital reserves exceeding $150,000 and are comfortable with $112,500 exposure
- The founding team believes that a higher-quality Pilot 1 will command better investor terms (higher valuation, lower dilution)
- Competitive pressure demands a faster, more polished launch
- The founding team is capable of sustained high-intensity execution during Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1

**When to prefer S3-O2-I2 instead:**
- Founder capital reserves are below $150,000
- The founding team values capital preservation over marginal quality improvements
- The market timing is not urgent enough to justify the extra spend
