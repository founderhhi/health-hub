# Scenario S3-O1-I1 — Our Level 1 (Bootstrapped) / Investor Level 1 (Bootstrapped)

## Scenario Summary

Maximum-constraint early-investor path. The founders self-fund at bootstrapped intensity (~40% of ideal spend) through Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1 only (~5-7 months), then a bootstrapped-level investor arrives right after Pilot 1 completes (approximately M7-M8). Both parties operate at bare-minimum budgets throughout. The earlier investor entry compared to S2 reduces founder capital exposure significantly (~$21,000-32,000 vs. $36,000-50,000 in S2-O1-I1), but double-bootstrapped budgets still create the longest S3 timeline and thinnest margins.

- **Economic cost to Production:** $105,000-135,000 / INR 8,715,000-11,205,000
- **Target timeline to Production:** 26-32 months
- **Founder/company capital before investor:** $21,000-32,000 / INR 1,743,000-2,656,000
- **Investor capital modeled:** $84,000-103,000 / INR 6,972,000-8,549,000
- **Equity structure:** 60 / 20 / 20 (founder / founding team / investor pool)
- **Main risk to watch:** bootstrapped investor capital barely covers Pilot 2 + Production at minimum staffing; if revenue underperforms, runway evaporates before break-even

## Recommendation

S3-O1-I1 is the **highest-risk combination** within Scenario 3. While the earlier investor entry (right after Pilot 1) reduces founder capital exposure to the lowest level in the entire 21-combination matrix ($21,000-32,000), the double-bootstrapped budgets create a fundamentally fragile path. The investor capital of $84,000-103,000 is insufficient to cover Pilot 2 and Production Readiness at even minimum staffing levels, as shown by the negative cash positions beginning at M17.

**This path should be avoided.** It suffers from three compounding problems:
1. **Insufficient investor capital** — bootstrapped investor funding cannot bridge the gap from Pilot 2 through Production at viable staffing levels
2. **Zero runway margin** — the M6-M7 transition has less than 1 month of cash, meaning any investor delay is immediately fatal
3. **Revenue dependency** — the model requires revenue to ramp faster than historical comparables for similar-stage health-tech platforms to avoid cash-out during Production Readiness

**If this is the only option available:**
1. The investor must commit at the high end of the range ($103,000) with funds available by M8
2. Revenue targets in Pilot 2 must be treated as hard gates, not aspirational
3. A contingency bridge round ($15,000-25,000) should be pre-arranged for M16-M18
4. Production Readiness scope must be aggressively compressed to 8 months maximum

**Consider S3-O1-I2 or S3-O1-I3 for meaningful risk reduction** — the same low founder capital exposure with substantially more investor capital.

## Assumption Notes

- Android is modeled from the external quote: 480 total hours at $15/hr average across the mobile team.
- Remaining web work is normalized to 150%-160% of Android effort, with an expected case of 745 hours.
- Salary benchmarks for employed coverage: INR 60,000 for doctors ($720/mo), INR 18,000 for admin ops ($217/mo), INR 25,000 for technical support ($301/mo).
- Our L1 (bootstrapped) spend is modeled at ~40% of ideal team utilization, yielding ~$3,800/mo founder contribution pre-investor (~$2,900 team + ~$900 ops/infra/overhead). Lower than S2-O1-I1's $8,900/mo because the self-funded window is shorter (7 months vs. 9 months) and must be sized to realistic bootstrapped constraints.
- Investor L1 (bootstrapped) contribution sized at $84,000-103,000, representing bare-minimum capital to attempt Production at constrained pace.
- Pre-Pilot duration compressed to 5 months (vs. 6 in S2) reflecting the urgency to reach investor conversation.
- All INR conversions at 1 USD = 83 INR per params.md.
- FX buffers of 10-15% applied to ETB and KES revenue projections.
