# Scenario S3-O3-I1 — Our Level 3 (Fully Funded) / Investor Level 1 (Bootstrapped)

## Scenario Summary

Structurally contradictory path. The founders over-invest at corporate intensity (~150-200% of ideal spend) through Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1, building a polished product and operational foundation — then a bootstrapped-level investor arrives right after Pilot 1 and contributes bare-minimum capital for scaling. The mismatch between the high-quality foundation and the under-funded scaling phase creates an awkward capital structure: excellent early execution followed by resource-starved growth.

- **Economic cost to Production:** $150,000-190,000 / INR 12,450,000-15,770,000
- **Target timeline to Production:** 22-26 months
- **Founder/company capital before investor:** $89,000-120,000 / INR 7,387,000-9,960,000
- **Investor capital modeled:** $61,000-70,000 / INR 5,063,000-5,810,000
- **Equity structure:** 60 / 20 / 20 (founder / founding team / investor pool)
- **Main risk to watch:** high founder capital exposure followed by insufficient investor capital to sustain the scaling phases; the early over-spend creates expectations the bootstrapped investor cannot fund

## Recommendation

S3-O3-I1 is **structurally contradictory and should be avoided.** The founders over-invest at corporate intensity ($89,000-120,000 over 3-5 months), building a high-quality foundation — then a bootstrapped investor contributes only $61,000-70,000, which is insufficient to sustain the operating level established during the fully funded early phases.

This creates a predictable failure mode:
1. The Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1 are excellent — high utilization, thorough hardening, comprehensive training
2. The investor arrives with bare-minimum capital
3. The team must dramatically reduce intensity to match the new capital constraint
4. The quality regression damages team morale, partner confidence, and operational consistency
5. Cash goes negative during Pilot 2, requiring emergency capital or scope cuts

**This path should only be considered if:**
1. The founders view the fully funded Pre-Pilot as an investment in product quality that will attract a better investor later (treating I1 as a bridge to a future I2/I3 round)
2. The bootstrapped investor brings strategic value (network, partnerships, market access) beyond their capital contribution
3. Revenue ramps faster than modeled, compensating for the under-funded investor

**In nearly all cases, S3-O2-I2 or S3-O3-I2 would be superior alternatives** — the first reduces founder exposure while properly funding scaling, and the second maintains the fully funded foundation with adequate investor capital.
