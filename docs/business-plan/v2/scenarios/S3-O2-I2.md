# Scenario S3-O2-I2 — Our Level 2 (Ideal) / Investor Level 2 (Ideal)

## PRIMARY RECOMMENDED PATH

## Scenario Summary

The balanced early-capital path. The founders self-fund at ideal intensity (~100% of baseline spend) through Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1, producing a credible live pilot with real patients, measured throughput, and operational data over ~6 months. An ideal-level investor arrives right after Pilot 1 (approximately M6-M7), providing properly-sized capital to fund Pilot 2 and Production Readiness at sustainable resource levels. Both parties invest at the baseline needed to meet milestones without excess. This combination produces the strongest investor narrative ("we built it, tested it with real patients, proved demand, and are raising to scale — here is our Pilot 1 data") while keeping founder capital exposure in a manageable range.

This is the recommended path for Health Hub because it optimizes across all six planning dimensions simultaneously: timeline, cost, founder exposure, dilution, risk, and narrative quality.

- **Economic cost to Production:** $170,000-215,000 / INR 14,110,000-17,845,000
- **Target timeline to Production:** 18-22 months
- **Founder/company capital before investor:** $53,000-65,000 / INR 4,399,000-5,395,000
- **Investor capital modeled:** $117,000-150,000 / INR 9,711,000-12,450,000
- **Equity structure:** 60 / 20 / 20 (founder / founding team / investor pool)
- **Break-even month:** 22-28
- **Risk level:** Medium
- **Recommendation:** **Pursue (Primary)**
- **Main risk to watch:** investor close timing — must happen within 45 days of Pilot 1 completion to maintain momentum and avoid a capital gap

## Why This Path

### The Core Logic

S3-O2-I2 is the intersection of four constraints:

1. **Minimum credible founder investment.** $53,000-65,000 over 6 months is enough to demonstrate genuine commitment to investors without bet-the-house exposure. At ~$10,000-13,000/month, the founder is funding a real team at real utilization — not a side project.

2. **Maximum investor data before capital deployment.** The investor enters after Pilot 1, meaning they see real patients, real consultations, real partner engagement, and real operational data before writing a check. This is the strongest possible proof point short of revenue traction.

3. **Right-sized scaling capital.** $117,000-150,000 funds 12-16 months of Pilot 2 + Production Readiness at ideal (not bloated) levels. The operational staffing ladder, infrastructure, and development team all run at the capacity needed to meet milestones — no more, no less.

4. **Timeline that respects both urgency and quality.** 18-22 months is fast enough to maintain team energy and competitive positioning, but slow enough to build the operational backbone (SOPs, training, compliance, partner management) that a healthcare platform requires.

### Why Not Other Paths

| Alternative | Why S3-O2-I2 Is Better |
|-------------|----------------------|
| S1-L2 (self-fund everything) | Costs the founder $155,000-195,000 vs. $53,000-65,000. Same risk profile but 3x the personal financial exposure. No investor network or validation. |
| S2-O2-I2 (investor later) | $18,000-30,000 more founder capital for 2-4 more months. The delay does not produce proportionally more investor leverage. |
| S3-O2-I1 (bootstrapped investor) | Same founder cost but investor under-funds scaling. Cash goes negative by M18. High risk of operational failure during the most critical growth phase. |
| S3-O2-I3 (fully funded investor) | $50,000-70,000 more total spend for 2-4 months of acceleration. Potentially exceeds the 20% investor pool. Good as an upside if Pilot 1 exceeds expectations, but not the base plan. |
| S3-O1-I2 (bootstrapped founder) | Under-invests in Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1, producing a weaker pilot result. The investor sees less traction and demands more equity or passes entirely. |

### Milestone Criteria (Detail)

Each phase gate has explicit pass/fail criteria. These are not aspirational — they are hard gates.

**Pre-Pilot Gate (M4):**
- Web platform: all 10 feature modules load without error; auth flow complete; SSR stable
- APK: core patient journeys functional (registration, dashboard, consult request)
- Ops: doctor training playbook v1 complete; admin callback SOP v1 complete; 2+ internal rehearsal cycles passed
- Business: investor pitch deck drafted; 3+ partner clinics signed for Pilot 1

**Pilot 1 Gate (M6):**
- Users: >200 active patients registered; >50 completed paid consultations
- Operations: callback response time <4 hours; no unhandled patient escalations; doctor utilization >40% during operational hours
- Technical: zero P0 bugs in production; uptime >97%; SSR stable under real load
- Revenue: >$400/month from real transactions (subsidized pricing acceptable)
- Investor: term sheet signed or in final negotiation with <30 days to close

**Pilot 2 Gate (M12):**
- Users: >2,000 active patients; >500 monthly consultations
- Operations: full staffing ladder active; callback SOP v2 in use; partner reporting automated
- Technical: APK feature-complete; web hardening 90%+ complete; load-tested for 5,000 concurrent users
- Revenue: >$3,000/month with clear growth trajectory
- Kenya: market research complete; 2+ potential partners identified

**Production Readiness Gate (M20):**
- Compliance: all regulatory documentation filed; partner-license model validated
- Security: penetration testing complete; OWASP Top 10 addressed; security incident response plan documented
- Operations: near-24/7 coverage achieved (3-shift model); all SOPs at v3+; support ticketing system live
- Release: release governance process documented and tested; rollback procedures validated
- Revenue: >$8,000/month; monthly revenue growth >10%

### Why Sustained Velocity Matters

In S3-O2-I1, dev team capacity drops from 480 to 200 hrs/mo when the investor enters — a 58% velocity cliff at exactly the moment scaling demands are highest. In S3-O2-I2, the investor funds the team at the same ideal rate the founders established, meaning:

- Feature development continues at Pre-Pilot pace throughout Pilot 2
- APK hardening and web security work happen in parallel, not sequenced
- Bug fixes and operational improvements do not compete with new features
- The team maintains rhythm and momentum instead of context-switching to survival mode

This sustained velocity is the single largest difference between I1 and I2, and it is worth the additional $33,500-55,000 in investor capital.
