# Scenario S2-O3-I3 — Our Level 3 (Fully Funded) / Investor Level 3 (Fully Funded)

## Scenario Summary

Maximum-investment path within S2. The founders self-fund at fully funded intensity (~150% of ideal spend) through Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1, deploying corporate-level resources to compress the early phases into 4-6 months. A fully funded investor arrives during the Pilot 1 to Pilot 2 transition (approximately M5-M7) and sustains corporate-level resourcing through Pilot 2 and Production Readiness. Both parties invest at maximum intensity, producing the fastest S2 timeline (16-20 months) and the highest total economic cost ($260,000-345,000). This scenario is functionally similar to S1-L3 in total spend but splits the capital burden and adds investor network value.

- **Economic cost to Production:** ~$303,000 / INR 25,149,000
- **Target timeline to Production:** 18 months
- **Founder/company capital before investor:** ~$124,000 / INR 10,292,000
- **Investor capital modeled:** ~$179,000 / INR 14,857,000
- **Equity structure:** 60 / 20 / 20 (founder / founding team / investor pool)
- **Main risk to watch:** over-capitalization of a pre-revenue company; spending $303,000 before product-market fit is confirmed carries significant downside if market assumptions prove wrong

## Recommendation

S2-O3-I3 is the **fastest and most expensive S2 scenario**, reaching Production in approximately 18 months at a total economic cost of ~$303,000. It should be **considered** only when both founders and investors have strong capital availability and conviction in the Health Hub thesis.

**This path works if:**
1. Founders can comfortably deploy $124,000-140,000 without personal financial strain
2. A fully funded investor ($153,000-205,000) is committed and willing to close by M6-M7
3. The team can sustain corporate-level intensity (150% utilization) for 19 consecutive months
4. There is a clear competitive or market-timing reason to prioritize speed over capital efficiency
5. Both parties accept that the full 20% investor pool will be consumed, limiting future raise flexibility

**When this scenario is appropriate:**
- A competitive threat (e.g., a well-funded health-tech entrant in East Africa) demands faster market capture
- The investor brings significant strategic value beyond capital (network, partnerships, regulatory access) that justifies the higher dilution
- Pilot 1 results exceed expectations and both parties want to capitalize on momentum
- Kenya market opportunity matures faster than expected, creating a dual-market scaling narrative

**When to avoid this scenario:**
- No clear competitive urgency; speed for speed's sake is wasteful
- Investor is purely financial with no strategic value; the dilution cost is not justified
- Founders cannot sustain $22,800/mo for 6 months without personal financial strain
- The team cannot maintain 150% utilization without burnout

**Compared to S2-O3-I2:**
- S2-O3-I3 is 4 months faster (18 vs 22 months) but costs $80,000 more
- Dilution increases from 15% to 20%, consuming the entire investor pool
- The speed advantage is meaningful only if there is a concrete reason to be in Production by M19 rather than M22

**Compared to S3-O2-I3 (the S3 upside recommendation):**
- S2-O3-I3 requires more founder capital ($136,800 vs $59,000) for a similar timeline
- S3-O2-I3 achieves comparable speed with less founder exposure
- S2-O3-I3 has a stronger investor narrative (more founder skin-in-game) but at a steep personal cost
- For most founders, S3-O2-I3 is the more capital-efficient way to achieve maximum speed
