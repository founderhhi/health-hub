# Scenario S2-O3-I1 — Our Level 3 (Fully Funded) / Investor Level 1 (Bootstrapped)

## Scenario Summary

Structurally contradictory path. The founders self-fund at fully funded intensity (~150% of ideal spend) through Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1, deploying corporate-level resources for 4-6 months, then a bootstrapped-level investor arrives during the Pilot 1 to Pilot 2 transition (approximately M5-M7). The mismatch is stark: the company builds momentum with high-intensity resourcing, then downshifts to a bootstrapped investor who cannot sustain that pace. Operations slow, team utilization drops, and the momentum gained in early phases is partially wasted.

- **Economic cost to Production:** ~$163,000 / INR 13,529,000
- **Target timeline to Production:** 26 months
- **Founder/company capital before investor:** ~$124,000 / INR 10,292,000
- **Investor capital modeled:** ~$39,000 / INR 3,237,000
- **Equity structure:** 60 / 20 / 20 (founder / founding team / investor pool)
- **Main risk to watch:** momentum collapse at Pilot 2 when investor capital is insufficient to sustain the operational tempo established during the founder-funded phases

## Recommendation

S2-O3-I1 is a **structurally contradictory scenario** and should be **avoided**. The core problem is a capital mismatch: the founders invest at corporate intensity ($22,800/month for 6 months = $136,800), building significant momentum and operational expectations, then a bootstrapped investor arrives with only $39,000 — barely enough to cover 3 months of even the reduced burn rate.

**This scenario fails for three specific reasons:**

1. **Cash flow is structurally negative.** The company goes cash-negative by M10 and never recovers within the planning horizon without additional capital injections. A $39,000 investor contribution cannot bridge 20 months of Pilot 2 + Production at any reasonable burn rate.

2. **Momentum whiplash.** The team operates at 150% intensity for 6 months, building features, onboarding partners, and establishing operational tempo — then abruptly drops to 50% utilization. This is psychologically destructive and operationally wasteful.

3. **Misaligned investor incentives.** An investor contributing $39,000 to a company that has already deployed $136,800 in founder capital has very different risk exposure and return expectations. The governance and reporting overhead of managing this relationship may exceed the capital value.

**If the founders have the capital for O3 spending, they should pair with an I2 or I3 investor (see S2-O3-I2 or S2-O3-I3) to maintain momentum through scaling phases. Alternatively, they should reduce their own spend to O2 and pursue S2-O2-I2 for a more balanced trajectory.**
