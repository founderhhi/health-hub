# Scenario S3-O2-I1 — Our Level 2 (Ideal) / Investor Level 1 (Bootstrapped)

## Scenario Summary

Compressed founder phase with constrained investor follow-on. The founders self-fund at ideal intensity (~100% of baseline spend) through Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1, building a credible prototype and live pilot with real patients over ~6 months. A bootstrapped-level investor arrives right after Pilot 1 (approximately M6-M7), providing minimum capital to bridge Pilot 2 and Production Readiness. The ideal-level self-funding produces a strong Pilot 1 result, but the bootstrapped investor capital creates resource tension during the scaling phases.

- **Economic cost to Production:** $125,000-160,000 / INR 10,375,000-13,280,000
- **Target timeline to Production:** 24-30 months
- **Founder/company capital before investor:** $53,000-65,000 / INR 4,399,000-5,395,000
- **Investor capital modeled:** $72,000-95,000 / INR 5,976,000-7,885,000
- **Equity structure:** 60 / 20 / 20 (founder / founding team / investor pool)
- **Break-even month:** 30-36
- **Risk level:** High
- **Main risk to watch:** bootstrapped investor capital barely covers Pilot 2 + Production at constrained pace; strong Pilot 1 creates expectations that under-funded scaling cannot meet

## Recommendation

S3-O2-I1 is a **high-risk combination** that creates an asymmetric trajectory: a strong, well-funded Pre-Pilot and Pilot 1 followed by a resource-constrained scaling phase. The ideal-level founder investment produces a credible Pilot 1, but the bootstrapped investor capital is insufficient to sustain the operational staffing ladder through Production Readiness. Cash goes negative by M18, creating a hard dependency on revenue ramp or bridge capital.

**This path is viable but fragile.** It should only be pursued if:
1. No higher-investment investor options are available after Pilot 1
2. The founding team is confident that revenue will ramp faster than conservative projections
3. A bridge funding mechanism (founder loan, revenue-based bridge, or second small round) is available as a safety net
4. The team accepts that the dev team will drop to ~40% utilization after the investor enters, significantly slowing feature development

**If the revenue ramp is slower than modeled or the investor close slips past M7, this scenario fails.** Consider S3-O2-I2 for meaningfully lower risk, or S3-O2-I3 for faster execution with proper resourcing.
